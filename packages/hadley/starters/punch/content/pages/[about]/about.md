I am Noa Varela. I photograph the city I live in, mostly early in the morning and late in the afternoon, when the
light lies low enough to draw on the walls. In between I design books and posters for small publishers.

I learned photography in a darkroom, and the portraits I return to most are Berenice Abbott's, from Paris in the
twenties. She began as Man Ray's darkroom assistant and was soon photographing the Left Bank herself: James Joyce,
Jean Cocteau, the writers and painters around them. A postcard of one of those portraits hangs above my enlarger.
